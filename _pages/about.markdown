I am 朱虹翱 (pinyin: ZHŪ HÓNG ÁO), a second-year PhD student in computational linguistics at UCSD, advised by <a href="https://alexwarstadt.github.io" target="_blank">Alex Warstadt</a>. Before that, I completed my undergraduate degree in Linguistics at Shanghai Jiao Tong University (SJTU), Shanghai, China.

### Research
I work in computational linguistics and computational psycholinguistics. I use language models to model how people learn and process language, and I ask what these models can and cannot tell us about learnability.

Current work at UCSD:

- **Critical periods.** People who start learning a language late rarely reach native-like proficiency, but language models show no such limit. I am studying what it would take for models to show these age effects.
- **What makes a construction learnable.** Children learn English passives after actives. Using language models trained on manipulated data, I test whether frequency or morphological complexity explains this, in English and in Māori.
- **Surprisal and reading in Chinese.** When does language model surprisal predict Chinese reading times, and why do scaling results differ across eye-tracking corpora? See our <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04898" target="_blank">preprint</a>.

Before UCSD, I worked with <a href="http://huhailinguist.github.io/" target="_blank">Hai Hu</a> at SJTU on how language models acquire the Chinese *Ba* construction and on <a href="https://aclanthology.org/2026.tacl-1.34/" target="_blank">ZhoBLiMP</a>, a benchmark of Chinese minimal pairs; with <a href="https://www.asc.ohio-state.edu/schuler.77" target="_blank">William Schuler</a> at Ohio State on language model surprisal as a predictor of human reading times; and with Lei Lei on detecting AI-generated text with Burrows's Delta.

I co-organized the first [ChineseBabyLM Challenge]({% post_url 2026-07-12-ChineseBabyLM %}) (NLPCC 2026), a Chinese counterpart to BabyLM, and I organize the UCSD Linguistics BrownBag and the LeM🍋N Lab meetings.
